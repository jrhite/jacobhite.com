The tools I will use to save instructional time, track trends in student behavior,
keep lines of communication open with families, and give students leadership
roles.

- **Google Classroom.** The central hub for agendas, assignments, slides, and
  lesson recordings, both in person and online. Posting everything in one place
  saves instructional time because students always know where to look, and
  guardian summaries keep families informed.
- **GitHub Classroom.** Distributes starter code and collects student work with
  full version history. I can see each student's progress between classes and
  spot who is stuck before they fall behind. Trained student "tech leads" help
  classmates with Git, giving them a leadership role.
- **Google Forms.** Runs the student background survey, exit tickets, and quick
  wellbeing check-ins. The data shows me who understood the lesson and who needs
  a private check-in, and it works the same way online.
- **Classroomscreen.** Displays visual timers, work symbols, and a random name
  picker on the projector. Timers make transitions predictable, and the name
  picker gives every student an equal chance to contribute.
- **Pear Deck.** Interactive slides let every student answer at once,
  anonymously if needed, during instruction. Quieter students and English
  learners can participate without speaking in front of the class, in person or
  online.
- **Google Sheets behavior and engagement tracker.** A simple log of positive
  notes, missing work, and help requests. Reviewing it weekly helps me spot
  trends, such as a student who suddenly stops submitting work, so I can respond
  early and share specific examples with families.
